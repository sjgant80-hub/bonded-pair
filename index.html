The Bonded Pair is an honest lab that trains two small models coupled on each other's state and forced to disagree, then measures them against an equal-capacity monolith and an independent ensemble across many random seeds. You run the experiment in-browser and see the accuracy of each system and their decision boundaries. The reproducible finding: two small models beat one small model, but the coupled bonded pair beats neither a plain ensemble nor a same-size monolith.

The honest result. Two small heads beat one small head — but that's the generic ensemble effect, and a plain average of two independent nets gets it. The §17 coupling (each sees the other's state) + forced disagreement (negative-correlation learning) beat neither a plain ensemble nor a same-capacity monolith on these tasks. A bigger monopole is still a monopole — and here it wins. That's a clean negative result, and shipping it straight is the point: the estate's whole thesis is proof-of-play over theatre. Gate 14/14 (it encodes the negative so it can't be quietly forgotten — if a future coupling ever beats the monolith, the gate flips and shouts).
Honest scope. Tiny 1-hidden-layer nets on 2D tasks, capacity-controlled, deterministic (seeded). It does not disprove paired architectures in general — the open frontier is tasks that need genuine iterative mutual inference, which these feedforward tasks don't.
